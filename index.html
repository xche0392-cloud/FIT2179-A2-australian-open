🎾 Australian Open

The Grand Slam of Asia-Pacific

By Xengyu Chen · 2026

The Australian Open is the first Grand Slam of the year, held every January in Melbourne, Australia. Since its inception in 1905, it has grown into one of the most prestigious tennis tournaments in the world, attracting over one million spectators annually and players from more than 70 nations.

This visualisation explores five key questions: How has the tournament grown? Who comes to compete? Who has dominated? How do Australians perform at home? And who truly owns Melbourne Park?

Act 1: What is the Australian Open, and why does it matter?

The Australian Open has undergone a remarkable transformation over the past two decades. Once overshadowed by Wimbledon and the US Open, it has emerged as the most-attended Grand Slam in the world. Two indicators tell this story clearly: the crowds keep growing, and the money keeps rising.

Annual Attendance (2000–2026)

Total spectator attendance has more than doubled since 2000, peaking above 800,000 in recent years — more than any other Grand Slam.

Men's Singles Winner's Prize Money (2000–2025)

Prize money awarded to the men's singles champion has grown nearly fivefold since 2000, reaching USD $3.5 million in 2025. The dip in 2021 reflects COVID-19 pandemic restrictions on prize distribution.

Act 2: A Truly Global Stage

The Australian Open draws competitors from every corner of the globe. In 2026, players representing over 30 nations competed at Melbourne Park. The map below shows the geographic spread — darker shading indicates more players from that nation.

Top 10 Nations by Player Count (AO 2026)

The United States leads all nations in player appearances at AO 2026, followed closely by Italy and Australia. The host nation's strong representation reflects the depth of domestic tennis development.

Europe — particularly Spain, France, and Italy — remains the heartland of world tennis. However, the strong showing from the United States and Australia signals a broader shift in the sport's global balance of power.

Act 3: Who Has Ruled the Australian Open?

Since 2000, the Australian Open has been defined by a small number of extraordinary players. On the men's side, Novak Djokovic's dominance is unparalleled — no player in the Open Era has won Melbourne as many times. On the women's side, Serena Williams set the standard, though a wider range of nations have claimed the title.

Men's Singles Champions (2000–2026)

Each dot represents a title. Djokovic's column stands alone — 10 titles, spread across 15 years of dominance.

Women's Singles Champions (2000–2026)

The women's draw has seen greater variety, with champions from the United States, Belarus, Belgium, Japan, and Kazakhstan all claiming titles in this period.

Combined Titles by Country (Men's and Women's, 2000–2026)

Serbia leads the men's count through Djokovic alone, while the United States leads the women's tally by a wide margin. The stacked bars show how each nation's total is split between genders.

The contrast between men's and women's dominance is striking. Serbia's entire tally comes from one player — Novak Djokovic. The United States, by contrast, has produced multiple women's champions across different generations.

Act 4: How Do Australians Fare on Home Soil?

Playing in front of a home crowd at Melbourne Park is a unique privilege — but does it translate into results? In 2026, Alex de Minaur was the standout Australian performer, reaching the quarterfinals before falling to world number one Carlos Alcaraz. Most other Australian players were eliminated in the opening rounds, reflecting the brutal depth of the modern draw.

Australian Players' Best Results (AO 2026)

De Minaur reached the QF (Round 5), while most compatriots exited in R64 or R128. The gap between De Minaur and the rest of the Australian contingent is immediately visible.

Australian Players by Home State

New South Wales produces the most Australian Open players (7), followed by Queensland (6). Victoria — home of Melbourne Park itself — contributes only 2, showing that proximity to the tournament does not guarantee tennis success.

Act 5: The Legends — Who is the True King or Queen of Melbourne?

Titles alone do not tell the full story. To truly compare the legends of Melbourne Park, we look at three dimensions: titles won, win rate across all matches played at the Australian Open, and years of dominance. All scores are normalised — a value of 1.0 means the best in that category among this group of six legends.

Legend Stats Comparison (Normalised)

Darker cells indicate stronger performance. Djokovic and Serena Williams score a perfect 1.0 across all three dimensions. Federer and Graf follow closely in win rate but fall short on titles and years of dominance.
